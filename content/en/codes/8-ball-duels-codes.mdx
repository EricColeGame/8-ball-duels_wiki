## Mastering Cue Ball Physics and Shot Selection

Having the best cue purchased with promo rewards will not win matches if your fundamental pool mechanics are lacking. Competitive duels come down to two primary skills: shot execution and cue ball control.

### Controlling the Break
The break shot dictates the tempo of the entire rack. Avoid hitting the front ball at maximum power with zero spin, as this often causes the cue ball to rebound unpredictably and scratch into a side pocket. Instead, apply a slight touch of backspin and hit the head ball slightly off-center to scatter the rack while leaving the cue ball dead in the center of the table.

### Planning Three Shots Ahead
Never shoot a ball without knowing where your cue ball will stop for the subsequent shot. Before taking your stroke:
- Identify your target object ball.
- Determine the angle required to pocket it cleanly.
- Calculate where the cue ball will travel along the tangent line after collision.
- Use top-spin (follow) or bottom-spin (draw) to alter the cue ball's path away from hazards.

### Managing Problem Clusters
During open racks, take mental note of tied-up balls. If two stripes or solids are wedged against the rail or each other, plan a breakout shot early in the game while multiple open balls are still on the felt. Leaving a difficult cluster for your final shot before the 8 ball almost always leads to a turn handover.
